Modelling notes
===============

Hopfenberg and Hixson-Crowell are not independent candidates. Hopfenberg,
Q = 100[1 - (1 - k t)^n], reduces exactly to Hixson-Crowell at n = 3. In the
original formulation n is fixed by the geometry of the eroding device
(1 slab, 2 cylinder, 3 sphere) and is not a quantity to be estimated. Fitting
it freely, as is common, makes the model degenerate: on data that Hixson-
Crowell describes well the optimiser drives n to the upper bound and the two
models return identical residual sums of squares. Both are kept in the
candidate set here because both appear in the published literature, but the
duplication is reported rather than hidden, and ties in R2 are broken in
favour of the model listed first, which is the simpler one. That convention
is generous to R2 and therefore makes the conclusions of the study
conservative.
